# Evidence and writing guardrails

## Never invent

Do not invent or silently infer:

- sample sizes, exclusions, effect sizes, uncertainty, P values, or FDR values;
- experimental conditions, protocols, software versions, or database releases;
- ethics approvals, consent, funding, conflicts, contributions, accessions, or repository links;
- citations, DOI metadata, author lists, or publication details;
- figure panels or validation experiments.

## Claim ladder

Match verbs to evidence strength:

- Description: `was observed`, `differed`, `was enriched`, `was reduced`.
- Association: `was associated with`, `correlated with`, `was linked to`.
- Prediction: `predicted in held-out data` only with a valid evaluation design.
- Mediation: use only after an explicit mediation analysis with assumptions stated.
- Causation: `caused`, `mediated`, `drove`, or `protected` only when the experimental design directly supports the claim.

Downgrade unsupported wording rather than adding speculative evidence.

## Result integrity

- Preserve the author's units and denominator.
- Reconcile inconsistent values before finalization.
- Keep adjusted and unadjusted analyses distinct.
- State when a result is exploratory, post hoc, or underpowered.
- Do not hide null or contradictory findings needed to interpret the main claim.

## Citation integrity

- Verify bibliographic facts before adding a reference.
- Prefer primary sources for specific experimental claims.
- Use reviews for broad framing, not as substitutes for original evidence.
- Never cite an exemplar article merely because its structure was analyzed.
- Do not copy phrases from exemplar papers.

## Placeholder policy

Use:

```text
[AUTHOR INPUT REQUIRED: specific missing item]
```

Do not use vague placeholders such as `TBD`, `xxx`, or a question mark that could be mistaken for final text.
